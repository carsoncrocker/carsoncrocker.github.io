Erdős Problem #188

Color every point of the plane red or blue, with one rule: no two red points may be exactly 1 apart. Now look for a blue run: points on a straight line, each exactly 1 from the next, all blue. How long a blue run are you forced to have? Erdős asked for the smallest k such that some coloring has no blue run of k points.1

6 ≤ k ≤ 6330→7 ≤ k ≤ 60

Package and note: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23151059. Computer-checked; not yet reviewed by a mathematician.

What was known

Tsaturian proved in 2017 that every such coloring has a blue run of 5, so k is at least 6.2 In June 2026 Currier, Mody, Xie and Zhang built a coloring with no blue run of 6330, so k is at most 6330.3 That left a wide gap: 6 ≤ k ≤ 6330.

What is new

  • Lower bound, k ≥ 7. Every red/blue coloring of the plane has two red points exactly 1 apart or a blue run of 6.4
  • Upper bound, k ≤ 60. An explicit coloring has no two red points exactly 1 apart and no blue run of 60.4

To my knowledge neither bound was in the literature before; the note asks to be corrected if it was.4

The idea in simple words

The coloring (k ≤ 60)

The red part is made of small, nearly round red patches (regular 24-sided polygons), each less than 1 across, repeated across the plane like wallpaper. One repeating tile holds 90 patches in 48 different sizes.4 Any two patches are either far apart, so every distance between them is more than 1, or close together, so every distance is less than 1. Either way, no two red points are exactly 1 apart.4

The hard part is showing that every run of 60 hits red, in every direction and from every starting point. Because the pattern repeats, it is enough to check one tile. A program splits the directions into 40,000 small slices and, for each slice, proves that 60 shifted copies of the red set cover the whole tile, with every step checked in rigorous ball arithmetic.4

The framework of red patches less than 1 across comes from Currier, Mody, Xie and Zhang, whose patches were hexagons of one size.3 Gabriel Currier suggested using red cells of different sizes, with smaller ones in the gaps. That idea is what brought the bound down from 85 to 69, then 64.5 Letting small patches sit very close to a larger one, together with further search, took it to 60.4

The lower bound (k ≥ 7)

This follows the method of Arman and Tsaturian, who proved the matching statement in three dimensions.6 Suppose a coloring had no red pair 1 apart and no blue run of 6. First, four computer-checked lemmas show it also has no red pairs at distances 4, 3, 2 and 6. Each lemma is a yes/no puzzle on about 9,000 to 12,000 exact points, and a SAT solver proves the puzzle has no solution, writing out a proof file (a DRAT certificate) that standard tools can check.4

Then a short argument by hand finishes it. Take a red point A. In any direction, one of the six points at distances 1 to 6 from A must be red, and distances 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 are ruled out, so the point at distance 5 is red. That makes the whole circle of radius 5 around A red, and that circle contains two points exactly 1 apart. Contradiction.4

How it was checked

  • No red pair 1 apart: checked by two separately written programs in exact arithmetic.7
  • No blue run of 60: proved by one program (Method 2) and confirmed by a second, separately written program with a different method (Method 1).7 A third verifier, written separately from the coloring data and the README’s description of the cells, also passed, and it found an exact blue run of 59, so 60 is the best this coloring can give. A separate attack program found no counterexample.8
  • Lower bound: the four proof files pass drat-trim and lrat-check, and also cake_lpr, a formally verified proof checker. A separate verifier rebuilt the puzzles with its own arithmetic and its own solvers.9
  • Outside AI referee: ChatGPT reproduced the checks and signed off on 7 ≤ k ≤ 60.10
  • One clean run: the whole package passed in one uninterrupted run from a clean copy (about 43 minutes).11

All the verifiers were written with the same AI tool (Claude Code), in separate sessions. That is not independence in the sense of different authors, which is why checks by others are very welcome.12 To my knowledge, no mathematician has checked the result yet.

How to check it yourself

Download the package from doi:10.5281/zenodo.23151059, unzip it, and in Linux or WSL run:7

pip install numpy numba python-flint
bash verify_all.sh          # upper bound, about 20-25 min on 8 cores
bash verify_all.sh full     # adds Method 1 and the lower bound (needs drat-trim), about 40 min

The last line says PASS or FAIL. The README in the package explains every step and what each program checks.

What is not claimed

  • The exact value of k. It is somewhere from 7 to 60.
  • Anything about k ≥ 8. The note records some conditional steps toward it; they are not a proof.7
  • Peer review. The note is posted with the package; it is not a journal paper.
  • That the search programs that found the coloring are part of the proof. They are not; only the certificates and verifiers are.4
