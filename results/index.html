Results

What has been proven, and where each result stands.

Five results are computer-checked proofs that improve a published bound on an open problem, and each has a page that explains it in plain English. One further result is a written proof that depends on a theorem in OpenAI's recent preprint. The two quantum-code results were established by exhaustive searches using two separately written programs; neither has an independently checkable certificate. Every result comes with a full package, with a DOI, that anyone can download and re-check; the three graph results also have a short check-it-yourself kit on their pages.

Published

Exact distances of five quantum codes (two posted earlier by DistQLDPC)

published 14 Oct 20264

A quantum code's distance is the fewest qubit errors that can corrupt the stored data without being detected. Cain et al. (2026) printed upper bounds of 16 and 20 for two codes, and Xu et al. (2024) printed 16 and 20 for two more; for the second of those, Webster et al. later found a logical of weight 16. For Panteleev and Kalachev's Example 4, decoder searches found nothing below 20, but no bound was proved. An exhaustive computer search gives the exact values. For the two Xu et al. codes, Yu-Fang Chen's public DistQLDPC repository had already posted distance 16 on 11 Oct 2026, three days before our record, which wrongly called all five values new (correction). Our values for those two agree with theirs and are an independent recomputation. It is a search result, not an independently checkable certificate, and not yet reviewed by an expert.

Exact distance of IBM's [[360,12]] code (independent recomputation)

published 13 Oct 20265

The [[360,12]] bivariate bicycle code of Bravyi et al. at IBM (Nature 2024) was printed with distance at most 24. Gideon Lee's public code database bicycle-race listed it as exactly 24 on 11 Oct 2026; our record of 13 Oct wrongly called the value new (correction). Ours is an independent recomputation: an exhaustive search, checked by a second program written separately, plus an explicit weight-24 logical. A search result, not an independently checkable certificate.

d ≤ 24 (printed)→d = 24
doi:10.5281/zenodo.23405904qLDPC board note

Chromatic number of 3D space (conditional)

published 10 Oct 20263

Color all of space so that no two points exactly 1 apart share a color. How many colors are needed? Assuming one theorem from OpenAI's proof that the plane needs at least 6, at least 7. A written proof, not a computer certificate, and not yet reviewed by a mathematician. For 4D and up, James Davies proved stronger results unconditionally two days earlier.

χ(ℝ³) ≥ 6→χ(ℝ³) ≥ 7, conditionally
doi:10.5281/zenodo.23273952Davies, arXiv:2610.12301

In progress

Every result moves through five stages. The five computer-checked results are at stage 3; the conditional 3D note is public with a DOI.

1. FoundThe system found a candidate and it passed a first rigorous computer check.
2. VerifiedIndependent checkers, a formally verified checker or Lean proof where possible, hostile AI review, a literature check and a 48-hour hold.
3. PublicThe authors of the prior work are told first. Then the note and code go public with a DOI.#188, #189, 31, 228, 276
4. arXivA written paper on arXiv, ideally with a professional mathematician as co-author.
5. Peer-reviewedAccepted by a journal after independent review.

More results are in preparation. A result reaches stage 3 only after the authors of the work it builds on have had a chance to look, and its package has a DOI. A result is marked expert-checked when an independent mathematician has checked it; none has that mark yet.

Caught before publishing

Things that looked new or true at first, until the checks said otherwise. Each claim listed here was caught before release; in one case the underlying computation was later published as a reproduction of the known result. They are listed because catching them is the point. Two earlier postings we missed were found only after publishing; they are on the corrections page. Items about unpublished work will be added when that work is published.

  • A quantum code distance already found by Liang et al. (14 Oct 2026). The [[248,10]] code from the same 10,000-atom paper had been solved exactly in 2025 (arXiv:2503.03827). The novelty claim was dropped before release; the computation stays in the package as a reproduction. Evidence
  • A claimed 63 for #188 (2 Oct 2026). The search had graded itself on a sample of lines; the true figure was at least 68. Never announced. Evidence
  • A 5-chromatic unit-distance graph in a small disk (20 Sep 2026) was already beaten in the Polymath16 project in 2021. Evidence
  • A theorem overtaken by Tomon's paper (arXiv:2610.03517, 2 Oct 2026), found before release. Dropped. Evidence
  • Higher-dimension bounds overtaken by Davies's paper (arXiv:2610.12301, 8 Oct 2026), found by a literature check just before release. The 3D note was cut to dimension 3. Evidence
  • An exact value already proved by Cabello in 2012 (arXiv:1112.5149). Dropped. Evidence
  • Table entries first counted as new that followed from graphs Cherkashin, Kulikov and Raigorodskii had already published. Evidence
