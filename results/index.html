Results

What has been proven, and where each result stands.

Five results are computer-checked proofs that improve a published bound on an open problem, and each has a page that explains it in plain English. The sixth is a written proof that depends on a theorem in OpenAI's recent preprint. Each comes with a full package, with a DOI, that anyone can download and re-check; the three graph results also have a short check-it-yourself kit on their pages.

Published

Chromatic number of 3D space (conditional)

published 10 Oct 20263

Color all of space so that no two points exactly 1 apart share a color. How many colors are needed? Assuming one theorem from OpenAI's proof that the plane needs at least 6, at least 7. A written proof, not a computer certificate, and not yet reviewed by a mathematician. For 4D and up, James Davies proved stronger results unconditionally two days earlier.

χ(ℝ³) ≥ 6→χ(ℝ³) ≥ 7, conditionally
doi:10.5281/zenodo.23273952Davies, arXiv:2610.12301

Erdős Problem #188

published 7 Oct 20261

Color the plane red and blue with no two red points exactly 1 apart. How long a straight blue run, with points 1 apart, are you forced to have?

6 ≤ k ≤ 6330→7 ≤ k ≤ 60

Erdős Problem #189

published 7 Oct 20261

Color the plane with two colors. Must some rectangle of area exactly 1 have four corners of one color? Yes, so the fewest colors that avoid such rectangles is at least 3.

r ≤ 21, only the trivial r ≥ 2→3 ≤ r ≤ 21
Explanationdoi:10.5281/zenodo.23178517

In progress

Every result moves through five stages. The five computer-checked results are at stage 3; the conditional 3D note is public with a DOI.

1. FoundThe system found a candidate and it passed a first rigorous computer check.
2. VerifiedIndependent checkers, a formally verified checker or Lean proof where possible, hostile AI review, a literature check and a 48-hour hold.
3. PublicThe authors of the prior work are told first. Then the note and code go public with a DOI.#188, #189, 31, 228, 276
4. arXivA written paper on arXiv, ideally with a professional mathematician as co-author.
5. Peer-reviewedAccepted by a journal after independent review.

More results are in preparation. A result reaches stage 3 only after the authors of the work it builds on have had a chance to look, and its package has a DOI. A result is marked expert-checked when an independent mathematician has checked it; none has that mark yet.

Caught before publishing

Things that looked new or true at first, until the checks said otherwise. None of these were ever published; they were caught before anything went public. They are listed because catching them is the point. Items about unpublished work will be added when that work is published.

  • A claimed 63 for #188 (2 Oct 2026). The search had graded itself on a sample of lines; the true figure was at least 68. Never announced. Evidence
  • A 5-chromatic unit-distance graph in a small disk (20 Sep 2026) was already beaten in the Polymath16 project in 2021. Evidence
  • A theorem overtaken by Tomon's paper (arXiv:2610.03517, 2 Oct 2026), found before release. Dropped. Evidence
  • Higher-dimension bounds overtaken by Davies's paper (arXiv:2610.12301, 8 Oct 2026), found by a literature check just before release. The 3D note was cut to dimension 3. Evidence
  • An exact value already proved by Cabello in 2012 (arXiv:1112.5149). Dropped. Evidence
  • Table entries first counted as new that followed from graphs Cherkashin, Kulikov and Raigorodskii had already published. Evidence
