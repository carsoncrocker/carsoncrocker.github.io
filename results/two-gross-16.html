Two-gross code: a 16-fault logical error in the depth-8 syndrome cycle

The two-gross code is a larger relative of IBM's gross code. Its code distance is 18, which means 18 qubit errors are needed to damage the stored data unnoticed. But the circuit that reads out the checks can itself fail, and failures inside it can do the same damage with fewer faults. In my reconstruction of the standard depth-8 circuit, I found sets of 16 circuit faults that no detector notices and that flip a logical qubit. So the circuit-level distance of that circuit is at most 16.12

Published reference: Fail fast reports circuit distance ≤ 18 for its BB(18)-circuit→My result: in my reconstruction, circuit distance ≤ 16 for this cycle and for every cycle in the defined uniform depth-8 family

Stage 3, published 21 Oct 2026; latest version 3.2: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23875528.1 An upper bound only. No human expert has reviewed it.

The problem in plain words

The two-gross code [[288,12,18]] is the larger code in IBM's planned modular architecture.3 Its code distance 18 was proved by Rowshan and Devitt.4 The circuit-level distance is a different number: the fewest faults, anywhere in the syndrome-measurement circuit (gates, idles, preparations, measurements), that trigger no detector yet apply a nontrivial logical operator. A single circuit fault can spread to several data qubits, so the circuit-level distance can be lower than the code distance.5

The circuit studied here is the depth-8 cycle of Bravyi et al., applied to the two-gross code.5 I use the round schedule of Section 2.4 of IBM's Fail fast paper: 18 noisy cycles followed by one fault-free cycle.6 Fail fast's Table 2 reports an upper bound of 18 for its BB(18)-circuit model, the weight of the lowest-weight logical operator its search found.6 I have not established that its full circuit and initialization conventions match my reconstruction.1

What is new, and what is not

This deposit, in my reconstruction: explicit sets of 16 faults, listed gate by gate, that cause an undetected logical error for X-type and Z-type faults under each of the two circuit-label readings documented in MAPPING.md. Version 2 supplies certificates of an upper bound of 16 for each of the 22,608 cycles in the uniform family defined and enumerated in the deposit: one ancilla per check, CNOT gates only, period 8, separate preparation and measurement steps, and one shared CNOT order and timing for each check type. The claim includes the deposit's initialization, boundary and noise conventions.1

Prior work: the code's use in the modular architecture, its code distance 18 and the depth-8 cycle come from the cited papers.345 Fail fast supplies the noisy-round and final fault-free-round schedule used here.6

Not claimed: no lower bound; nothing about cycles outside the uniform family (for example check-dependent orders, longer depth, extra ancillas or other gate sets); nothing about logical error rates or decoders. A published benchmark of distance solvers uses a different two-gross circuit and noise model, so its numbers are not comparable.7 Whether the 2,333 class certificates that use data idle faults still give 16 without idle noise is not claimed.1

"In my reconstruction" is meant literally. I rebuilt the circuit from the published tables and the repository source, and I have no confirmation that it is the circuit IBM used. The deposit's MAPPING.md lists, gate by gate, what the match rests on.1 My model begins with no errors on any qubit, as the simulation code of Bravyi et al. does.15 This initialization is my modeling choice for this reconstruction; Fail fast's Section 2.4 states no initialization convention.6 Versions 3 and 3.1 of the deposit incorrectly attributed this ideal initialization to Fail fast in MAPPING.md. Version 3.2 corrects it, adds no result and changes no result, and credits Victor Albert, who pointed it out in his comment on the Error Correction Zoo pull request.8 His comment was not a verification of my reconstruction or of the 16-fault bound.

How it was checked

  • Four propagators agree on a 16-fault set: the search pipeline's builder, a blind rebuild written from the papers only, a hostile-review rewrite, and the checkers in the package. Each runs the faults through the circuit and finds no detector firing and a flipped logical.1
  • Matrix sizes: two separately written circuit builds reproduce the detector and fault-column counts in Fail fast's Table 1 (2,736 detectors).16
  • A check readers can follow: HAND_CHECK.md lists the 24-qubit residual error and an 18-qubit operator that commutes with every check but anticommutes with the residual. With a residual of zero syndrome, those relations show a nontrivial logical error, and the file explains how to check them.1
  • The family claim: one script replays a certificate on all 22,608 cycles with no search; another re-derives the list of cycles from all 6!²×8 candidates. Removing one fault from a witness makes a detector fire in every case tested, which tests the supplied witnesses but excludes no other, smaller logical error; deliberately altered inputs are rejected.1
  • Re-check on 1 Nov 2026: From a fresh copy of v3.2, check_witness.py and check_family.py both passed, the second on all 22,608 cycles. I did not re-run check_enumeration.py.9

All four propagators were written by Claude, under my direction, in separate sessions, with ChatGPT as a reviewer. A misreading of the papers shared by all of them would survive. No human expert has verified the circuit, the witnesses or the certificates. I am not a professional mathematician or physicist. Corrections are welcome.1

Check it yourself

Download the package from doi:10.5281/zenodo.23875528, unpack it, and in its folder run:1

python3 -m venv venv
venv/bin/python -m pip install -r requirements.txt    # numpy and stim only
venv/bin/python check_witness.py        # the 16-fault sets; about 2 seconds
venv/bin/python check_family.py         # all 22,608 certificates; about 1 minute
venv/bin/python check_enumeration.py    # the cycle list is complete; about 1.5 minutes

Each prints a line ending in PASSED or FAILED. Run times depend on the machine and its load. HAND_CHECK.md explains the witness calculation.1
