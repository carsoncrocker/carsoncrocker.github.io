What happened, and when.

Dated entries, newest first, including the mistakes. Each links to its evidence. Dates are US Central time unless marked UTC.

October 2026

  1. 19 Oct 2026Published an explicit faithful unit-distance graph in R3 with 5,942 vertices, girth 5 and chromatic number 5. De Grey asked (Geombinatorics 35(3), 2026) whether 5-chromatic unit-distance graphs exist in R3 with any girth; Tomon's Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.4 (arXiv:2610.03517v2) give a stronger existence result, and this is one explicit, checkable example of girth exactly 5. Two separately written checkers certify the coordinates with interval arithmetic and check that all points are distinct and no non-adjacent pair is at distance 1. No claim is made that 5,942 is the minimum. The search, construction and checking programs were produced with Claude Code under my direction, and a review used ChatGPT; no human expert has reviewed it. Evidence: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23483410.
  2. 19 Oct 2026Published certificates for n + 2 equilateral points in ℓpn at eight pairs (p, n), including 31 points in ℓ829 and 55 points in ℓ553, all bearing on Kusner's question (Guy 1983) of whether an equilateral set can have more than n + 1 points. Swanepoel, Ge, Xu and Zhou, Chalmers (58 points in ℓ556), Xiong, and Chalmers and Ziegler had earlier proved or constructed related results. Three separately written checkers use different arithmetic. No claim is made that these dimensions are the least possible. The search, certificates and checking programs were produced with Claude Code under my direction; no human expert has reviewed it. Evidence: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23462743.
  3. 18 Oct 2026Two Zenodo records updated with credits. The description of the 228-vertex sphere graph (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23241819) now credits Chandan Kumar Shukla's preprint, posted on 8 October 2026, the same date as our deposit, which reports a 231-vertex vertex-critical 5-chromatic graph with 938 edges on this sphere (doi:10.20944/preprints202610.0589.v1); the sentence about Voronov et al. was reworded to say they noted their 372-vertex graph stays 5-chromatic after deleting any single vertex. The version 1.0 record of the five-code distances (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23379787) now carries a note crediting Yu-Fang Chen's DistQLDPC and pointing to version 1.1. Evidence: correction, correction.
  4. 18 Oct 2026An outside check. On 18 Oct, Alex Towell posted three audits, submitted as open pull requests, on plasma-ai/erdos issue #62, done with substantial help from OpenAI Codex. With his own checking programs, they corroborate the Erdős #189 two-color certificate (PR 7) and both parts of the #188 upper bound: Claim A, no two red points exactly 1 apart (PR 8), and Claim B, no blue run of 60 (PR 10). As he states, they are not blind (he read our packages first), they are substantially AI-assisted, and they are not human peer review or kernel verification. They do not cover the #188 lower bound or the #189 Lean proof. Thank you, Alex. The #188 and #189 pages now link his audits.
  5. 18 Oct 2026Published lower bounds on measurable chromatic numbers in dimensions 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12 and 14 that are above those in Table 1 of James Davies's preprint (arXiv:2610.12301), for example at least 54 colors in dimension 6 where his table gives 52. The method adds a few inequalities, mostly the triangle inequality, of the kind DeCorte, de Oliveira Filho and Vallentin used earlier to Davies's linear program. Each bound has an exact certificate accepted by three checking programs, one written blind, and also rests on a lemma proved in the accompanying note; certificates for six dimensions from 15 to 23 have passed two of the three. The same numbers bound the ordinary chromatic number only if Davies's Theorem 1.1, which is not yet refereed, holds. Not yet reviewed by a mathematician. Evidence: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23452776.
  6. 18 Oct 2026Published circuit-level distance 10 for the syndrome circuit of IBM's [[144,12,12]] gross code, for 12 noisy rounds followed by 2 noiseless ones, in both memory bases. Bravyi et al. proved the upper bound 10 and conjectured the value; Cross, He, Rall and Yoder reported 10 from numerical evidence; the compiled circuit files and the MaxSAT control values are from Aghababaie Beni, Higgott and Shutty and the tesseract-decoder project; Webster, Jacob and Higgott earlier filtered the detector error model by detector type. Two separately written programs, sharing the same mathematics and inputs, searched exhaustively and found no undetected logical error from 9 or fewer faults; explicit sets of 10 faults cause one. There is no independently checkable certificate, and no expert has reviewed it yet. Evidence: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23452754.
  7. 18 Oct 2026An overdue credit. On 11 October 2026, Koshelev, Kupavskii and Voronov submitted “Ordinary and measurable colorings of Euclidean three-space” (arXiv:2610.18907). Assuming OpenAI's Theorem 2.3, they prove the stronger statement that the ordinary and measurable chromatic numbers of three-dimensional space are equal, with both between 7 and 15. We noticed their paper on 15 Oct but did not add it to this site until now. The 3D cards on the home and results pages now cite it.
  8. 17 Oct 2026A second correction of the same kind. For two of the five codes in our 14 Oct record, Xu et al.'s [[714,100]] and [[1020,136]], Yu-Fang Chen's public DistQLDPC repository had already posted distance 16 on 11 Oct. Their values and ours agree; ours are an independent recomputation. Our prior-work check for that release covered papers, the qLDPC challenge board and one benchmark repository, and missed DistQLDPC's entries; other code databases were added to the check on 15 Oct. The results page and the Zenodo record now credit his entries. Evidence: correction.
  9. 15 Oct 2026A corrected version (v5) of the Erdős #188 note.   10. 15 Oct 2026A correction.   11. 14 Oct 2026Corrected 17 Oct: two of the five values had been posted three days earlier; see the 17 Oct entry. Exact distances for five quantum error-correcting codes that had been published without a proved distance: two from Cain et al.'s paper on running Shor's algorithm with 10,000 atoms, two from Xu et al. (2024), and one example of Panteleev and Kalachev. Two separately written exhaustive-search programs establish the distances, with explicit logical operators attaining them; there is no independently checkable certificate, and no expert has reviewed it yet. For the [[248,10]] "bb18" code from Cain et al., Liang et al. had already found the exact distance in 2025, so we did not claim it as new; it stays in the package as a reproduction. Three of the Cain et al. authors were emailed on 14 Oct. Evidence: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23379787, caught before publishing.
  12. 14 Oct 2026A lesson about writing: an AI-written draft can be correct and still hard to read. Drafts now get a separate readability pass, with the result and the main idea first, computation logs in an appendix, and ChatGPT used as an editing aid.
  13. 13 Oct 2026Corrected 15 Oct: the value was already posted; see the 15 Oct entry. Exact distance 24 for the [[360,12]] quantum code of Bravyi et al. at IBM (Nature 2024). Two separately written programs agree. The first version also listed the [[360,16]] and [[360,20]] codes from a later IBM paper as new; each is two disconnected copies of a smaller code, so those were not new results, and version 1.2 corrected this on 13 Oct.
