Log

What happened, and when.

Dated entries, newest first, including the mistakes. Each links to its evidence. Dates are US Central time unless marked UTC.

October 2026

  1. 10 Oct 2026Posted the 3D argument to the Polymath16 thread, where the Hadwiger–Nelson group is working through OpenAI's paper, and asked for the step from the plane to 3D to be checked. Evidence: the thread itself.
  2. 10 Oct 2026Published a short note extending the first step of OpenAI's new proof that the plane needs at least 6 colors to higher dimensions. Assuming one theorem from their preprint, it gives that 3D space needs at least 7 colors; the best known bound was 6. Before release, ChatGPT, Gemini and separate Claude reviewers checked the argument, and a last literature check found a paper by James Davies, posted two days earlier, that proves stronger results for 4D and up without OpenAI's theorem, so the note was cut to the 3D case and credits him. Not yet reviewed by a mathematician. Evidence: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23273952.
  3. 8 Oct 2026The three verified results were published on Zenodo with full packages that re-check with one command: the 31-point triangle-free graph in 3D (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23241767), the 228-point graph on a sphere (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23241819) and the 276-point graph in the hyperbolic plane (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23241840). Evidence: the deposits themselves.
  4. 7 Oct 2026Three more results passed a full re-check and are now listed as verified; their Zenodo packages are coming: a 31-point triangle-free 5-chromatic graph in 3D, a 228-point 5-chromatic graph on a sphere and a 276-point 5-chromatic graph in the hyperbolic plane. Each page has files to check it yourself. Evidence: ledger.
  5. 7 Oct 2026Erdős #188 (7 ≤ k ≤ 60) and Erdős #189 (3 ≤ r ≤ 21) were published on Zenodo with their proof files and code: #188, #189. This site went up the same day. Evidence: ledger, ledger.
  6. 6–7 Oct 2026Before publishing, #188 was checked again from every side: a formally verified proof checker (cake_lpr) accepted the lower-bound proofs, a third verifier written separately confirmed the upper bound and found an exact blue run of 59 (so 60 is the best this coloring can do), and the whole package passed one uninterrupted clean run. Evidence: ledger, ledger, ledger.
  7. 6 Oct 2026The #189 result now has a Lean 4 proof with no gaps, and its SAT proof also passed cake_lpr. Evidence: ledger, ledger.
  8. 5 Oct 2026An overnight run found the #189 certificate: 180 points that cannot be two-colored without a one-color rectangle of area 1. It answers a question Hu and Liu asked in September 2026. Evidence: ledger, ledger.
  9. 4 Oct 2026#188 reached k ≤ 60, certified by one program and confirmed by a second, separately written one. The last steps came from a storm of parallel questions on 3 Oct, which produced the idea of letting a small red cell sit very close to a larger one. Evidence: ledger, ledger.
  10. 2 Oct 2026The #188 search reported 63, but the rigorous checker found blue runs of at least 68: the search had learned to score well on its own shortcut test. From then on the checker does the scoring. It was never announced. Evidence: caught before publishing.
  11. 1–2 Oct 2026Gabriel Currier suggested using red cells of different sizes for #188. With them the bound went to 69, then 64. Evidence: ledger, ledger.
  12. 29–30 Sep 2026#188: the upper bound went from the published 6330 to 242, then 166, 107 and 85 in two days. Evidence: ledger, ledger.
  13. 27 Sep 2026#188: lower bound k ≥ 7, by four SAT certificates and a short argument by hand. Evidence: ledger.
  14. 21 Sep 2026On an earlier problem whose result is not public yet, Claude twice recommended stopping. Each time I asked it to keep going, and the work continued. The same day, asking "do we have the right rules" turned into the 7-point release checklist every result now goes through. Evidence: log quotes, checklist.
  15. 20 Sep 2026A certified result that looked new was found, within minutes, in a 2021 Polymath16 thread. Since then a literature check comes before any compute. Evidence: caught before publishing.
  16. 15 Sep 2026Started. The first attempts, aimed directly at a famous problem (Hadwiger–Nelson), closed without a new result. Evidence: ledger.

The searches, code and checks behind these results were produced with AI tools (Claude Code, with ChatGPT as a reviewer) inside a process I designed and ran. Corrections are welcome and will be dated.
